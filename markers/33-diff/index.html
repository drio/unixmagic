diff

Compare two files and show exactly what changed

diff compares two files line by line and shows what was added, removed, or changed. Doug McIlroy and James Hunt wrote it at Bell Labs, and it shipped with Fifth Edition Unix in 1974. Its output, the patch, became a universal unit of change.

Before diff, reconciling two versions of a source file meant reading them side by side. diff automated that, and once Larry Wall’s patch(1) arrived in 1985, you could mail someone a diff and they could apply it to their own copy of the file.

Hunt and McIlroy described the algorithm in a 1976 paper. It finds the longest common subsequence of the two files’ lines, which is expensive to compute naively, and they made it fast enough for the hardware of the time. Most tools today use Eugene Myers’s 1986 algorithm instead; it’s the default in both GNU diff and git.
