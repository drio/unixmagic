Daemons

Background processes that do the system's chores

Daemons are long-running background processes, usually started at boot, that do the system’s chores: cron runs scheduled jobs, syslogd collects log messages, sshd accepts logins. On Unix they are ordinary processes, not part of the kernel, so you can stop, restart, or replace a service without rebooting the machine.

The name comes from MIT’s Project MAC around 1963. Fernando Corbató’s team borrowed it from Maxwell’s demon, the imaginary being in physics that quietly sorts molecules, for background processes that work tirelessly on system chores. Unix daemons usually advertise it with a trailing d.
